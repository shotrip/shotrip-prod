## 🏛️ Introduction

* **Overview:** In the Ainu language, the name means "muddy river," "river mouth where driftwood piles up," or "plain at the bending river end." It is the village with the smallest population in Hokkaido.
* **Geography & Climate:** The Teshio River flows through the center of the village, which serves as a traffic junction where Route 40 and Route 275 split. It is one of the heaviest snowfall areas in Hokkaido, with an average annual snow accumulation exceeding **120 cm**. Mount Otoine-Fuji stands as a geographical landmark.
* **Local Products:** Buckwheat (soya-soba), white asparagus, and fruit tomatoes are the primary agricultural products of the region.

## 📍 Highlights & Landmarks

### 🚉 Otoineppu Station
![Otoineppu Station](/images/prefectures/northern_hokkaido/otoineppu_station.webp)
* **Overview:** The central transport hub of the village. The building features a retro station house design, an indoor soba shop, and an attached historical exhibition room.

### 🌾 Soba Fields (Summer Season)
![Soba Fields](/images/prefectures/northern_hokkaido/otoineppu_soba_field.webp)
* **Overview:** Large farming fields growing local buckwheat. The fields are located along the JR Soya Main Line tracks and can be seen from inside the train between Sakkuru Station and Teshio-Gawa-Onsen Station.

### 🗺️ The Naming Place of Hokkaido
![The Naming Place of Hokkaid](/images/prefectures/northern_hokkaido/naming_place_of_hokkaido.webp)
* **Historical Significance:** Located on the Teshio River floodplain in Azamonomanai. During the late Edo period, explorer Matsuura Takeshiro surveyed the Teshio River basin. At this specific location, he listened to an Ainu elder, which provided the conceptual basis for the name "Hokkaido."
* **Current Site:** The location functions as a canoe port. The JR Soya Main Line runs on the opposite bank of the river, and the wooden historical monument can be seen from the train windows.

### 🚂 Tempoku Line Museum
* **Overview:** Located inside the station hub (Otoineppu Station). It displays photographs, information panels, and a historical diorama of the Otoineppu Station yard during its peak operational years to document the Tempoku Line, which closed in 1989 after 75 years of service.

### 🪵 Mokuyukan Woodworking Center
* **Overview:** A public workshop facility for woodcraft creation, allowing visitors to build items ranging from small wooden goods (taking 2 to 4 hours) to larger furniture pieces (requiring several weeks or months).

## 🥢 Local Gourmet

### 🍜 Otoineppu Soba
![Otoineppu Soba](/images/prefectures/northern_hokkaido/otoineppu_soba.webp)
* **Overview:** The famous black station soba. It matches the Rishiri kelp broth incredibly well. Actually, the broth is so delicious that I definitely want you to drink all of the soup. By the way, this is where noodle and railway fans travel from all over the country just to eat it. It is very famous among the fans, and because it is privately owned, I recommend going to eat right when it opens. (It operates 4 days a week on Friday, Saturday, Sunday, and Monday, starting from 11:00 AM.)

## 💡 Author’s Tips

The total time needed for sightseeing in Otoineppu is about half a day to a full day. It feels just right if you base yourself in Sapporo or Asahikawa, leave early in the morning, and return as a day trip. The Soya Main Line, where Otoineppu Station is located, is one of Japanese top local railway lines, and the view from the train window running along the Teshio River is spectacular. However, because it is located in an incredibly rural area, the number of trains is extremely small. Therefore, you should first decide exactly which train you will take and when, and then plan your travel schedule around which sightseeing spots to visit.
