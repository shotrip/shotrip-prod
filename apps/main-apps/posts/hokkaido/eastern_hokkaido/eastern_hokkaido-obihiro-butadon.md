## 🏛️ Introduction

* **Origin & History:** Created in 1933 by Shuji Abe, the first owner of the restaurant "Pancho." The dish was inspired by the sweet soy glaze of grilled eel (*Unagi no Kabayaki*) to provide a highly nutritious meal. The dish spread to dining halls throughout Obihiro City during the 1950s and 1960s. Today, specialized pork slices and pre-made sauces are widely sold in local supermarkets, making it a standard household dish in the Tokachi region.
* **Cooking Varieties:** Local pork bowls are divided into two distinct technical styles:
  * **Grill Style (*Amiyaki*):** Grilled over an open net over charcoal. This process removes excess fat, resulting in a smoky and cleaner taste.
  * **Pan-Fry Style (*Frying Pan*):** Cooked directly in a flat pan. This locks in the meat juices, resulting in a richer, juicier texture.
* **Flavor Profiles:** The standard sauce uses a sweet, soy-sauce base, with individual restaurants adding distinct ingredients. Variations using miso base or salt base (*Shio*) are also available.

## 🥢 Recommended Butadon Restaurants

### 🐷 Butadon no Hanatokachi
* **Overview:** A specialized pork bowl shop using Hokkaido-sourced pork and additive-free homemade sauce. The signature item is the "Hambara Butadon," which includes both pork loin (*Loin*) and pork belly (*Bara*) slices. It is served with grated wild horseradish (*Yamawasabi*) from the Tokachi area.

### 🐷 Tokachi Butadon Ippin
* **Overview:** Established in 2000, this specialty restaurant uses Hokkaido pork loin. The cooking style involves repeated dipping in cold-aged sauce while grilling over charcoal. The standard pork bowl is topped with sliced white leek (*Shiraga-negi*). Various set menus are available.

### 🐷 Obihiro Hageten
* **Overview:** A long-running regional restaurant established in 1934 that specializes in tempura and local cuisine. The pork bowl recipe relies on a secret base sauce co-developed over 70 years ago by the first-generation chef, Shiroku Yano, alongside local cooks. The kitchen utilizes marbled Hokkaido pork loin, basted multiple times during net-grilling.

## 💡 Author’s Tips

The visual presentation alone looks so delicious that you might want to eat a lot, but the pork bowl itself is quite voluminous. Unless you are confident in your ability to eat huge portions, please be careful. I think most people coming to Hokkaido assume they will travel around other areas, not just Obihiro. Every single area has way too many attractive local foods. If you eat big portions at every single stop, you won't be able to enjoy the food you eat later from the bottom of your heart (though it is fine if you are confident in your stomach!). That said, giving up on Butadon is not an option! I have traveled around Hokkaido many times, and among all the dishes, Butadon easily ranks in my top three favorites. Skipping Butadon when sightseeing in Hokkaido—especially in the Eastern Hokkaido district—is absolutely not allowed! (haha) Of course, if you cannot eat pork for religious reasons, please don't worry about it!
