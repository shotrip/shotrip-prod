## 🏛️ Introduction

* **Location:** Deep within the rugged mountainous terrain of Shikoku lies a legendary, hidden region that feels completely isolated from modern Japan: the "Oboke and Iya" area in Tokushima Prefecture.
* **History:** In the 12th century, defeated samurai (the Heike clan) fled here from their pursuers and established a self-sufficient life in this genuine "Lost World."
* **Iconic Landmark:** The symbol of this refuge is "Iya no Kazurabashi," a thrilling suspension bridge woven from wild shirakuchi-kazura (hardy kiwi vines). With every step, the bridge creaks and sways, revealing the raging torrent far below through the wide gaps underfoot—an ultimate defensive survival system designed to be cut down in an instant if enemies attacked.
* **Experience:** Wrapped in green forests and mountain mist, a spiritual adventure that makes you completely forget today's concrete jungles begins right here.

## 📍 Highlights & Landmarks

### 🏘️ Ochiai Village View
![Ochiai Village View](/images/prefectures/tokushima/oboke_ochiai_village_view.webp)
* **Historical Context:** Ochiai Village is located on a mountain slope near the confluence of the Iya and Ochiai rivers in Higashi-Iya. The settlement spans a steep incline with a vertical elevation difference of approximately 390 meters. Local oral traditions link the area to Heike clan refugees and early pioneers.
* **Preservation Status:** The area retains residential buildings constructed from the mid-Edo period to the early Showa period, along with terraced stone walls and agricultural fields. It was designated as a National Important Preservation District for Groups of Traditional Buildings in 2005.

### 🌉 Oku-Iya Double Vine Bridges (Oku-Iya Niju Kazurabashi)
![Oku-Iya Double Vine Bridges](/images/prefectures/tokushima/oboke_oku_iya_double_vine_bridges.webp)
* **Overview:** This site features two parallel vine suspension bridges, historically referred to as the "Husband and Wife Bridges" (Meotobashi), associated with the historical Heike clan.
* **Facilities:** The designated grounds include an operational campground situated next to the local forest and river.

### 🏛️ Higashi-Iya Museum of History and Folk Culture
* **Historical Exhibits:** The museum details the regional history of the Iya area, focusing on the Genpei War era, Taira no Kiyomori's lifetime, and the correlation between the *Tale of the Heike* and local Heike refugee legends using photographic panels and documentation.
* **Cultural Artifacts:** Displays cover the distinct local traditions, seasonal lifestyles, changes in historical daily tools, and regional artistic items preserved by the village residents.

### 🌊 Iya no Kazurabashi & Biwa Falls
![Biwa Falls](/images/prefectures/tokushima/oboke_biwa_fall.webp)
* **Background:** The Iya region is historically documented as an isolated settlement where Taira no Kunimori and Emperor Antoku's followers established roots after the Battle of Yashima. Due to geographic isolation from external transport links until modern times, medieval lifestyles and customs have been preserved.
* **The Bridge:** Suspended over the Iya River, this 45-meter-long bridge is designated as a National Important Tangible Folk Cultural Property. It is constructed from harvested shirakuchi-kazura vines and features an open-slat floor design over the gorge.

## 💡 Author’s Tips

A 2-day, 1-night trip (on the premise of renting a car at Kotohira) is highly recommended. There are simply so many things to see. It is no exaggeration to say that this region embodies old-school, good old Japan to the fullest, so everything that catches your eye is bound to become a highlight. Furthermore, many of the lodging facilities are Japanese-modern ryokan and hotels, so I highly encourage you to stay overnight as part of the overall experience. (*Note: If you absolutely must rely only on public transportation, local buses run from Oboke Station, making it possible to visit a select few spots. If you just want a quick taste of Oboke, it is also feasible to base yourself in Okayama, Takamatsu, or Kochi City, take the limited express train to Oboke Station, and do some light sightseeing via the local bus.*)

---

## ⚠️ A Request from the Author

This is a deep mountainous area, and even where stations exist, many of them only see a few trains stop per day. The nearest large town is Kotohira, which is also home to Mount Konpira. Therefore, the safest approach is to travel by rail up to Kotohira, and enter the Oboke area by renting a car from there. (*If you try to secure a vehicle directly at Oboke, there is a high possibility you will end up stranded without a rental car.*) The roads are steep mountain passes, so please drive with extreme caution.
