## 🏛️ Introduction

Kure is a beautiful port town facing the Seto Inland Sea. This is the very place where the legendary battleship "Yamato"—built under the highest level of state secrecy in Japan—was born.

At the Yamato Museum, the symbol of the city, a 1/10th scale replica of the Yamato stretching 26 meters long is beautifully reconstructed. Here, you can truly experience the history of their almost-insane level of craftsmanship and design technology.

However, Kure's charm goes beyond the weight of its heavy history. As dusk falls, the "Yatai Street" comes alive along the harbor with glowing red lanterns. This marks the beginning of a deep street hack where you can taste local foods like "Kure Reimen" (cold noodles) and "Navy Curry," which was originally created by old navy cooks.

### 🏛️ Yamato Museum
![Yamato Museum](/images/prefectures/hiroshima/kure_yamato_museum.webp)
* **What to See:** 
  * A huge 1/10th scale replica of the famous battleship Yamato.
  * Real historical pieces on display, including a Zero fighter plane and a Kaiten human torpedo.
  * Exhibits showing Kure's history of modern shipbuilding and steelmaking technology.

### ⚓ Iron Whale Museum (JMSDF Kure Museum)
![JMSDF Kure Museum Monument](/images/prefectures/hiroshima/kure_iron_whale_museum.webp)
* **What to See:**
  * The actual "Akishio" submarine used by the Japan military until 2004. You can go inside and explore.
  * Displays of real underwater mines and ocean-clearing equipment.
  * A nice gift shop and cafe where you can buy official navy-themed items.

### 🌊 Alley Karasukojima
![Ships at Alley Karasukojima](/images/prefectures/hiroshima/kure_alley_karasukojima.webp)
* **Highlights:**
  * A seaside walkway where you can see real submarines and navy ships docked right in front of you.
  * Old brick buildings and a historic crane that give the area a vintage, retro atmosphere.
  * **Best Time to Visit:** Go at sunrise or sunset to watch the flags being raised or lowered on the navy ships.

### 🚲 Tobishima Kaido Rental Bicycles
* **Highlights:**
  * A scenic cycling path that connects 7 different islands with 7 unique bridges.
  * Flat roads running right along the coastline with very few cars or traffic lights.
  * **Convenient Option:** You can pick up a bike on one island and drop it off at a different station or port (Advance booking required).

## 💡 Author’s Tips

* If your main goal is to tour around the naval port city on foot or by bus, a 1-day day trip based out of Hiroshima is perfect. On the other hand, if you want to enjoy cycling through the detached islands as well, a 2-day, 1-night trip is highly recommended. There are various cycling courses available, but if you have come all the way to Japan to see these stunning views, spending about half a day just riding around will give you the absolute best value for your time.
* If you are planning a day trip, make sure to keep an eye on the schedule for the JR Kure Line, as train frequencies can vary. For those traveling from Osaka or Kyoto, making the journey directly can take a lot of time due to the transfers, so from an author's perspective, starting out from Hiroshima is definitely the way to go. (By the way, the view from the Kure Line window along the coast is absolutely beautiful!)
