## 🏛️ Introduction

* While tourist spots in Kyoto are crowded with travelers from all over the world, you can jump onto the JR Kosei Line and ride north for 40 to 50 minutes to access an overwhelming world of water surrounded by silence and mystery. That is Takashima City in Shiga Prefecture.
* What makes this city known to the world is the grand torii gate of Shirahige Shrine, standing alone in the blue water of Lake Biwa. This view, which reminds people of Miyajima in Hiroshima, turns into a breathtakingly emotional and beautiful scenery at dusk.
* However, the true charm of Takashima City is not just the torii gate. This is an area where the water is exceptionally clean even within Lake Biwa. In summer, a special experience awaits you that ordinary tourists cannot do: enjoying camping and SUP (stand-up paddleboarding) by the lake while "hacking the torii gate in the water from above the lake."

## 📍 Highlights & Landmarks

### 🌲 Metasequoia Avenue
![Metasequoia Avenue](/images/prefectures/shiga/metasequoia_avenue.webp)
* **Specifications:** The avenue is about 2.4 kilometers long with around 500 Metasequoia trees. It is located on Prefectural Road Koaraji Makinozawa Line and serves as the approach road to Makino Highlands, matching the surrounding Nosaka Mountains.
* **History:** The Makino Town Fruit Production Association planted the first trees in 1981 as part of a school farm project. Local residents maintained and grew the trees, and the prefectural road later added more plantings to create the current size.
* **Biology:** Metasequoia is a tall deciduous tree in the Cupressaceae family. The Japanese name is Akebonosugi. Its appearance changes across the four seasons (spring green buds, deep summer green, autumn colors, and winter snow). It was selected as one of the "New 100 Scenic Street Trees of Japan" by Yomiuri Shimbun in 1994.
* **Safety Rules for Visitors:** Parking and stopping vehicles on the road near the trees is prohibited. Visitors must watch for cars and secure their footing when taking photos. The surrounding roads are used for daily life, so drivers must reduce speed and watch for pedestrians.
* **Local Access:** Take the Makino Kogen Line bus from JR Makino Station for approximately 6 minutes and get off at the "Makino Pickland" stop. Drivers can use the free parking lot at Makino Pickland.

### 🫐 Agricultural Park Makino Pickland
![Makino Pickland](/images/prefectures/shiga/makino_pickland.webp)
* **Overview:** An agricultural park where visitors can experience seasonal fruit-picking. It consists of three main zones.
* **Orchard Zone:** Offers picking experiences for cherries (early summer), blueberries, grapes, chestnuts, and sweet potatoes.
* **Roadside Landscaping Zone:** Features the Metasequoia Avenue running straight through the orchard.
* **Park Zone:** Centered around the Center House. It includes a cafe, a restaurant, a lawn area, and a ground golf course. Inside the Center House, visitors can buy souvenirs and handmade gelato.
* **Local Access:** Take the Makino Kogen Line bus from JR Makino Station for approximately 6 minutes and get off at the "Makino Pickland" stop.
* **Operations:** Closed on Wednesdays and during the New Year holidays. The schedule changes depending on the season, so check in advance.
* **Official Website:** https://pic-land.com/

### ⛩️ Shirahige Shrine
![Shirahige Shrine](/images/prefectures/shiga/takashima_shirahige_shrine.webp)
* **Structure:** Features a vermilion torii gate inside Lake Biwa, with the main shrine buildings located across National Route 161. The main hall has a square layout. It connects to the worship hall built in the Meiji period, creating a complex roof structure.
* **History:** It has a history of over 2,000 years and is the oldest grand shrine in the Omi region. The current buildings were constructed by Toyotomi Hideyori following the wish of his father, Toyotomi Hideyoshi, with Katagiri Katsumoto serving as the builder.
* **Deity and Prayers:** Dedicated to Sarutahiko no Mikoto. People visit to pray for long life, marriage, childbirth, good luck, academic success, traffic safety, and safe navigation. It also enshrines Jurojin, one of the West Omi Seven Lucky Gods.
* **Events and Assets:** The annual grand festival is held on September 5 and 6, featuring the "Naruko Mairi" ritual to pray for the safe growth of two-year-old children. The grounds contain a stone monument with poems written by Yosano Tekkan and Yosano Akiko.
* **Safety Rules:** Walking across National Route 161 in front of the shrine is prohibited. To photograph the torii gate inside the lake, visitors must use the observation platform named "Omi Shirahigedai" located in front of the shrine office.
* **Local Access:** Located approximately 3 kilometers from JR Omi-Takashima Station. Walking takes about 40 minutes one way. Rental bicycles are available in limited numbers at the tourist information center inside JR Omi-Takashima Station.

## 💡 Author’s Tips

* **Geography and Scenery:** The location is in the northwestern part of Shiga Prefecture, which holds Lake Biwa in its center as the water source for the Kansai region. While Shiga has a strong image of Lake Biwa, the beautiful tree-lined avenues and agricultural products unique to this fertile land are also points you cannot miss. You can enjoy the great view of Lake Biwa from the JR Kosei Line on your way to Takashima City, making the travel itself a great activity.
* **Time and Base:** You can visit the main spots in half a day. (Since it is a bit far to start from Tokyo, it is better to use Kyoto or Osaka as your starting point).
* **Transportation Choices:** This area has good community buses and rental bicycles, so you do not really need a rental car. However, there are many great driving spots around Lake Biwa and the Metasequoia Avenue, so renting a car to go around is also a good option. That said, my personal recommendation is the rental bicycle at Omi-Takashima Station. Just riding through the area feels amazing. (Though rental bicycle cannot be a option for Metasequoia Avenue because Omi-Takashima station is far from the spot.)
