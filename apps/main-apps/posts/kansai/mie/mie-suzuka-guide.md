## 🏛️ Introduction

* **Location and Scale:** Located in the north-central region of Mie Prefecture with a population of approximately 200,000. It is a major city in the prefecture, ranking third in population alongside Tsu and Yokkaichi.
* **Cultural Sphere:** Positioned in the Kansai region, but its cultural and economic ties are closer to the Nagoya metropolitan area.
* **Economic Profile:** Driven by a strong automotive industry and manufacturing sector, notably serving as the home of the Suzuka Circuit racing facility.
* **Regional Assets:** The area bordered by the Suzuka Mountains and the sea produces local specialties such as Ise tea, Satsuki azaleas, and traditional crafts.

### ⛩️ Tsubaki Grand Shrine (Tsubaki-Okami-Yashiro)
![Tsubaki-Okami-Yashiro](/images/prefectures/mie/tsubaki_okami_yashiro.webp)
* **Overview & History:** Located at the central base of the Suzuka Mountains in Ise Province. According to historical records of the shrine, it was established in the 27th year of Emperor Suinin (3 BC) by imperial decree of Yamato-hime no Mikoto, making it one of the oldest shrines in Japan. It serves as the head shrine (*Honqu*) among over 2,000 shrines nationwide that worship Sarutahiko Okami. The shrine name was designated using the kanji for "camellia" (*Tsubaki*) during the reign of Emperor Nintoku.
* **Enshrined Deities:** The main enshrined deity is Sarutahiko Okami (the earthly deity of guidance). The shrine also enshrines Ninigi no Mikoto and Takahatachichihime no Mikoto in the main sanctuary (*Aiden*), alongside Ame no Uzume no Mikoto and Konohanasakuya-hime no Mikoto in the subordinate sanctuary (*Haishi*).

## Local Gourmet

### 🍵 Seihoyen (Suzuka Hunter Branch)
* **Overview:** A long-standing retail store selling local Ise tea cultivated in Suzuka, including Kabusecha (shaded green tea), Hojicha (roasted green tea), and Sencha. It also stocks a wide selection of local confectionery items suitable for souvenirs.
* **Hours:** 10:00 - 16:00.
* **Closures:** Sundays.
* **Local Access:** A 5-minute walk from Hiratacho Station on the Kintetsu Suzuka Line.

## 💡 Author’s Tips

* Since you can fully enjoy yourself at Suzuka Circuit for a whole day, my personal recommendation is a 2-day, 1-night trip. While you could make use of a rental car at the station, there are many meals that go well with alcohol, so it might be a good idea to actively use taxis, rails, and buses instead.
* If you stay at an inn, you can also enjoy traditional Japanese cuisine. Suzuka is also close to Ise, so I think it would be nice to use one of the days for a pilgrimage to Ise Grand Shrine.
