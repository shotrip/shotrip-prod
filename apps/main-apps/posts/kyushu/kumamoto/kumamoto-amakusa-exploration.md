## 🏛️ Introduction

* **Dramatic History Stage:** Amakusa, a paradise of beautiful islands floating on the west coast of Kyushu. This is not just a peaceful resort area. It is the stage of a dramatic history found nowhere else in the world, where "Hidden Christians" risked their lives to keep their faith secret during the dark era of the 17th century when Japan completely banned Christianity.
* **Sacred Destination:** When you step into "Sakitsu Village," a quiet seaside town registered as a World Heritage site, you will see a beautiful Gothic-style church standing right in the middle of a calm fishing village—an exotic view that will totally glitch your brain. Let's start a journey to this sacred place that will push your intellectual curiosity to the limit, where the quiet beauty of the sea meets the memories of samurai who once prayed on tatami mats.

## 📍 Highlights & Landmarks

### ⛪ Sakitsu Church (Inside World Heritage Sakitsu Village)
![Sakitsu Church](/images/prefectures/kumamoto/amakusa_sakitsu_church.webp)
* **History & Architecture:** A Gothic-style church designed by Japanese architect Yosuke Tetsukawa. The current building was rebuilt in 1934 under French missionary Father Halbout. The inside features a rare tatami mat floor layout.
* **Location Significance:** At Father Halbout's strong request, the church was built on the exact site of the former Yoshida Shoya chief magistrate residence, where *Efumi* (stepping on religious images to detect Christians) was practiced during the ban on Christianity. The current altar stands on the spot where *Efumi* took place.
* **Designations:** The port area was named one of Japan's 100 Best Beaches in 1996, one of Japan's 100 Scented Landscapes in 2001, and the fishing village view became a National Important Cultural Landscape in 2011.
* **Booking from this page:** https://www.kyusanko.co.jp/ryoko/pickup/sakitsu-church/

### 🐘 Myokeura (Elephant Rock)
![Elephant Rock](/images/prefectures/kumamoto/amakusa_elephant_rock.webp)
* **Overview:** A coastal rock formation on the Amakusa West Coast. When viewed from the Jusanbutsu Park side, the main rock looks like an elephant. The site is a popular location for scuba diving and Stand-Up Paddleboarding (SUP). It became a National Scenic Spot and Natural Monument in 1935.

### ⛩️ Mount Kura & Kuratake Shrine
![Mount Kura](/images/prefectures/kumamoto/amakusa_mount_kura.webp)
* **Topography:** Located in the southeastern part of Amakusa Kamishima facing the Yatsushiro Sea, Mount Kura is the highest peak in the Amakusa Islands at **682 meters** above sea level.
* **Shrines:** Kokura Kannon rests on the mountainside, and Kuratake Shrine sits at the summit to pray for safe sailing. The stone torii gate at the peak stands open against the sky and is a popular photography location known as the "Torii in the Sky."

### 🐬 Dolphin Marine World
![Dolphin Watching](/images/prefectures/kumamoto/amakusa_dolphin_watching.webp)
* **Overview:** The sea off Itsuwa-machi is rich in fish, which attracts a resident group of wild Indo-Pacific bottlenose dolphins. Boat tours operate daily to watch these wild dolphins swim near the surface.

## 🥢 Local Gourmet Markets

### 🐙 Roadside Station Ariake Rippleland
![Roadside Station Ariake Rippleland](/images/prefectures/kumamoto/amakusa_ariake_rippleland.webp)
* **Facility Layout:** Located in Ariake-machi, the gateway to Amakusa. The site includes a souvenir shop, a restaurant, and the Ariake Onsen "Sazanami-no-Yu" hot spring facility.
* **Octopus Specialties:** The shop sells local octopus products like vacuum-packed *Tako-steak* (soft-boiled octopus) and *Takomeshi-Goo!* (octopus rice mix). The restaurant serves traditional *Takomeshi* (octopus rice), Octopus Tempura Bowls (*Tako-Tendon*), and seafood items like *Kaisendon*, raw fish sets, and Seafood Champon noodles topped with baby octopus, shrimp, and scallops.

## 💡 Author’s Tips

For a satisfying trip, the best length of time is 2 days and 1 night. You should base yourself in Kumamoto City or move your base to Amakusa, planning to stay overnight at a local ryokan, hotel, or spa resort. From seafood gourmet and hot springs to churches and amazing views created by islands and structures, there are endless things to see. (Even if you do not go inside the Sakitsu Church, the town area alone is well worth seeing.) Public transport inside the city is honestly hard to call well-developed, so you should rent a car as soon as you arrive in Amakusa to travel around the sightseeing spots.
