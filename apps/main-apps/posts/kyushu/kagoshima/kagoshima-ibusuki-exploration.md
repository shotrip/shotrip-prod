## 🏛️ Introduction

* Among the many hot spring towns in Japan, there is no other city where you can have such an aggressive and wild experience. That is "Ibusuki," located at the southernmost tip of Kagoshima Prefecture. The first thing travelers visiting here should experience is not taking off their clothes to soak in a hot spring... but rather being literally "buried alive" in the coastal sand while wearing a yukata. This is the world's only "Natural Sand Bath." High-heat hot spring components flowing under the coast warm up the entire beach. With the heavy weight of the sand and the geothermal heat, a waterfall of sweat pours from your whole body in just 10 minutes. Let's head out for the ultimate survival spa experience, completely detoxing your body and mind with the power of the Earth's magma while listening to the sound of the waves on the calm Kinko Bay stretching out right in front of you.

## 📍 Highlights & Landmarks

### 🏖️ Saraku Sand Bath Hall
![Saraku Sand Bath](/images/prefectures/kagoshima/ibusuki_saraku_sand_bath.webp)
* **Overview:** The largest sand bath facility in Ibusuki, featuring outdoor natural sand baths along the beach, large indoor hot spring baths, and saunas. Visitors use the outdoor beach during low tide and a roofed sand area during high tide or bad weather.
* **History:** The health benefits of these sand baths were recorded as early as 1843 in the local document *Sankoku Meisho Zue*. The water source is a sodium chloride spring with a very high temperature of **80°C**, creating visible steam on the beach when the tide goes out.
* **Official Website:** https://www.ibusuki-saraku.jp/en

### ⛩️ Hirakiki Shrine
![Hirakiki Shrine](/images/prefectures/kagoshima/ibusuki_hirakiki_shrine.webp)
* **Overview:** Historically known as the top shrine (*Ichinomiya*) of the Satsuma region. It is dedicated to traffic safety, sea navigation, and fishing protection. The site features traditional bright red shrine buildings. The treasure hall stores the *Matsu Ume Maki-e Kushige* (a decorated wooden comb box), which is registered as a National Important Cultural Property.

### 🌋 Cape Nagasakibana
![Cape Nagasakibana](/images/prefectures/kagoshima/ibusuki_cape_nagasakibana.webp)
* **Overview:** The southernmost cape of the Satsuma Peninsula. A curved sandy beach extends to the west, leading toward Mount Kaimon. On clear days, the southern islands of Yakushima, Takeshima, and Iojima are visible across the ocean. The walking path features a stone monument with poetry by Housaku Shinohara. The nearby Akamizubana area marks the northern limit for wild cycad growth and is a National Special Natural Monument.

## 🥢 Local Gourmet

### 🥚 Ontamaran-don Rice Bowl
* **Food Concept:** A local rice bowl dish that pairs a soft-boiled egg (cooked in natural sand-bath hot spring water) with regional Kagoshima ingredients like Berkshire pork (*Kurobuta*), bonito, or eel.
* **The Restaurant: Aoba**<br>A traditional restaurant focusing on Kagoshima products. The lunchtime favorite is the Ontamaran-don. They also serve Kurobuta shabu-shabu using local Ibusuki pork, raw Kuro-Satsuma chicken slices, and local seafood like silver stripe round herring (*Kibinago*) and bonito. At night, the restaurant offers local sweet potato shochu, regional craft beer, and local sodas.

### 🥢 Tosenkyo Flowing Somen Noodles
![Flowing Somen Noodles](/images/prefectures/kagoshima/ibusuki_flowing_somen_noodles.webp)
* **Food Concept:** A dining system where somen noodles spin in circular, water-filled tables. The noodles are chilled in cold spring water from a nationally recognized top water source. The dish uses a sweet dipping sauce made from Rishiri kelp and Ibusuki Yamagawa bonito flakes.
* **The Restaurant: Chojuan Kaimon-Tosenkyo**<br>This flowing somen noodle experience is a classic summer tradition in Ibusuki. During winter, they also serve warm options like seiro set meals.
* **Official Website:** https://minamibussan.jp/?pg=menu&id=252

## 💡 Author’s Tips

The best plan is a 2-day, 1-night trip to Ibusuki while basing yourself around Kagoshima-Chuo Station. Because it is a hot spring destination, trying out each spot takes time, and the number of trains on the Ibusuki-Makurazaki Line from Kagoshima-Chuo Station is quite small. If you do not stay overnight, your trip will be very rushed. Also, since the sightseeing spots are spread out across the city, it is much easier to rent a car in Ibusuki and enjoy your 2-day trip at your own pace. (Buses do run in some parts, but if you rely on them too much, you will have a bad time.) Plus, as the author, I think it is not a good option to visit a hot spring town and head to your base without staying the night!
