## 🏛️Introduction

The history of Kamo Aquarium, affectionately known as "Kamosui," developed through the following historical timeline:

* **1930:** The facility originated as the "Yamagata Prefectural Aquarium," built collectively through funding provided by local residents.
* **Wartime & Postwar Eras:** The facility adapted through shifting socio-political environments, being requisitioned by the Imperial Japanese Navy during World War II, and subsequently functioning as a campus building for a local fisheries high school to preserve its operations.
* **1964:** The aquarium executed a structural relocation and reconstruction process to what is now referred to as the former building.
* **1997:** A sharp decline in visitor attendance placed the aquarium's continuity in severe jeopardy. During this critical period, a microscopic jellyfish naturally appeared inside a coral display tank, turning the facility's focus toward jellyfish cultivation.
* **2000–2005:** The intensive research and creative display techniques led to the facility claiming the highest number of showcased jellyfish species in Japan by 2000, expanding to a globally leading status by 2005.
* **2014:** The institution executed a comprehensive full-scale renewal, officially transforming into a specialized jellyfish aquarium.

## 🪼 Landmarks & Highlights

![Jerryfish](/images/prefectures/yamagata/kamo_aquarium_jerryfish.webp)

The facility operates as an active biological exhibition and research center housing diverse marine organisms:

* **Jellyfish Research & Exhibition:** Kamo Aquarium currently maintains and researches approximately 100 distinct species of jellyfish. The facility partners with domestic and international universities, as well as marine museums, to analyze undisclosed ecological patterns and life cycles.
* **Marine Biodiversity Galleries:** The exhibits feature a wide range of aquatic life, including hidden fish native to coastal rocky reefs and pinnipeds (seals and sea lions) observed through their distinct behavioral patterns.
* **Interactive Programming:** Visitors can register for specialized field experiences at the 2F Information Desk, including paid backyard tours and pinniped feeding experiences.

## 🍽️ Gourmet

### Gyosho Dining Okimizuki
Tsuruoka City holds an official designation as a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy, supported by coastal ports that historically distributed seafood via the Kitamaebune merchant shipping networks. Chef Takeshi Suda, who holds the official title of Tsuruoka Food Honorary Ambassador, structures the on-site menu around the theme of local gastronomy to represent the regional culture, history, and landscape.
