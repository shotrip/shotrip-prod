## 🏛️ Introduction

* **Main God:** Dedicated to Takemikazuchi-no-Okami, the legendary deity of national foundation and martial arts.
* **Shrine Origin:** Traditional records date the foundation back to the inaugural year of Emperor Jimmu's reign.
* **Historical Significance:** Developed as a strategic military ritual base during the Nara and Heian periods, later receiving patronage as a deity of war from prominent commanders such as Minamoto no Yoritomo and Tokugawa Ieyasu.
* **Sacred Sword:** Associated with the legendary sword Futsunomitama-no-Tsurugi, documented in the *Kojiki* as a divine weapon used to establish regional stability.
* **Official Website:** https://kashimajingu.jp/
---

## 💡 Author's Tips

* **Geographical and Transit Characteristics:** Located in the southeastern sector of Ibaraki Prefecture. While transit planning within Ibaraki often assumes routing from Mito or Tsukuba, the Kashima region is functionally recognized by residents as adjacent to northeastern Chiba Prefecture. Consequently, overland routing via northeastern Chiba offers higher efficiency than routing from central or western Ibaraki points, a factor reflected in the regional transport network.
* **Alternative Return Routing:** An optional itinerary involves utilizing the Kashima Rinkai Railway to travel to the Mito district for regional commercial and culinary activities (such as purchasing Mito natto) before boarding a limited express service to return to the Tokyo metropolitan area.

### 🚆 By Train

* **Route A (Sobu Rapid / Narita Line):** Tokyo Station → Sobu Rapid Line → Chiba Station → Narita Line → Sawara Station → Kashima Line → Kashima-Jingu Station → Walk (10 minutes). Total transit time: Approx. 2 hours 30 minutes.
* **Route B (Sobu Main Line / Airport Narita):** Tokyo Station → Sobu Main Line (Airport Narita) → Narita Station → Narita Line → Sawara Station → Kashima Line → Kashima-Jingu Station → Walk (10 minutes). Total transit time: Approx. 2 hours 30 minutes.
* **Operational Constraints:** Kashima Line operations run at an approximate frequency of one train per hour, with transfer points varying by time schedule. Integrated smart cards (Suica, PASMO) are non-functional on the Kashima Rinkai Railway Oarai-Kashima Line between Mito Station and Kashima-Jingu Station.

## ⛩️ Landmarks & Highlights

### 🚪 Mitarashi Pond
![Mitarashi Pond](/images/prefectures/ibaraki/kashima_shrine_mitarashi_pond.webp)
The pond yields over 400,000 liters of spring water daily, featuring high clarity that allows a complete view of the pond bed. Historically, worshipers purified themselves at this site before performing religious rites. Currently, approximately 200 people gather here annually at the beginning of the year to conduct the midwinter purification ritual (*Daikan-Misogi*).

### 🌳 Botanical Grove (Juso)
![Botanical Grove](/images/prefectures/ibaraki/kashima_shrine_botanical_grove.webp)
A natural monument designated by Ibaraki Prefecture, comprising over 600 plant species. The primary canopy consists of mature *Cryptomeria japonica* (Japanese cedar), *Castanopsis* (chinquapin), *Machilus thunbergii*, and *Abies firma* (Momi fir), representing a botanical convergence zone for northern and southern growth limits.

### 🦌 Deer Park
![Deer Park](/images/prefectures/ibaraki/kashima_shrine_deer_park.webp)
An enclosure maintaining deer as sacred messengers, referencing the mythological role of Amenokaku-no-Kami during the transfer of land negotiations. Historical lore states that the spirits of Kashima Jingu were transported to Kasuga Taisha in Nara via deer transit, leaving derivative toponyms such as "Shishibone" in Edogawa Ward, Tokyo.

### 🌲 Sacred Tree (Goshinboku)
![Sacred Tree](/images/prefectures/ibaraki/kashima_shrine_sacred_tree.webp)
A *Cryptomeria japonica* specimen standing approximately 40 meters in height, with an estimated botanical age of 1,300 years, representing the oldest and largest flora within the immediate sanctuary perimeter.

### 🏮 Romon (Grand Tower Gate)
![Grand Tower Gate](/images/prefectures/ibaraki/kashima_shrine_romon.webp)
A 13-meter-high multi-story gate designated as an Important Cultural Property and classified as one of the three major tower gates in Japan. Commissioned in 1634 by Tokugawa Yorifusa, the first lord of the Mito Domain, to commemorate the recovery of the third Shogun, Tokugawa Iemitsu. The timber components were prefabricated by 130 carpenters at the Mito residence in Asakusa, transported via barge networks, and assembled on site. It received vermilion lacquer coats during the 1940 restoration and a copper-plated roof conversion during the 1970s.

### 🏢 Main Sanctuary (Honden, Ishi-no-ma, Heiden, Haiden)
![Main Sanctuary](/images/prefectures/ibaraki/kashima_shrine_main_sanctuary.webp)
An architectural compound consisting of four designated Important Cultural Properties donated by the second Shogun, Tokugawa Hidetada, in 1619.
* **Honden (Main Hall):** Situated at the rear terminal of the complex, structured in the Sangen-sha Nagare-zukuri style to house the primary deity.
* **Haiden (Worship Hall):** The front-facing structure utilized for formal ceremonial assemblies and matrimonial rites.

### 🚪 Grand Torii Gate (Otorii)
![Grand Torii Gate](/images/prefectures/ibaraki/kashima_shrine_otorii.webp)
Reconstructed on June 1, 2014, matching the exact dimensions of the previous granite torii gate that collapsed during the 2011 earthquake, utilizing mature cedar logs sourced from the shrine grounds.
