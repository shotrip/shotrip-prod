## 🏛️ Introduction

* **Temple Characterization:** Shibamata Taishakuten (formal name: Kyoeizan Daikyoji) is a historic Buddhist temple of the Nichiren sect that developed widespread popularity among Edo-period citizens through "Koshin" religious practices.
* **Central Religious Object:** The main object of worship is the "Taishakuten Itahonzon" (a wooden plaque). One side features the "Namu Myoho Renge Kyo" Buddhist inscription, and the reverse side features a carved image of Taishakuten.
* **Spatial Approach:** The entry path leading to the temple grounds is lined with traditional commercial shops and restaurants, forming a historical marketplace.

---

## ⏳ History & Origins

* **Foundation:** Established in 1629 (the Kanei era) by Zenna-in Nichichu Shonin, the 19th head of Shimousa Nakayama Hokekyo-ji Temple. His disciple, Nichiei Shonin, served as the primary builder of the facility.
* **Loss and Relocation of the Artifact:** The central wooden plaque was lost or hidden for an extended period during the middle of the Edo period. In 1779 (Ansei 6), during structural renovations of the main hall overseen by the 9th head monk Nikkio Shonin, the plaque was rediscovered on top of a structural beam.
* **The Koshin Connection:** Because the day of rediscovery matched the *Koshin* day on the traditional calendar, the temple formed a permanent administrative link with Koshin worship patterns.
* **Iconography Details:**
    * **Text Side:** Carved with central religious text flanked by a passage from the Lotus Sutra focusing on the elimination of illness.
    * **Image Side:** Displays an image of Taishakuten holding a sword in the right hand with an open left hand, symbolizing protection against historical natural disasters and fires.
* **Historical Distribution Network:** In 1783 (Tenmei 3), during a severe regional famine and plague outbreak, Nichikyo Shonin carried the wooden plaque across Tokyo and Chiba to distribute medicinal paper amulets (Ichiryufu) to affected populations, which established the temple's modern prominence.

## 🗺️ Access

### 🚆 Train Routing
* **Keisei Line:** Get off at Shibamata Station → Walk 3 minutes.
* **Hokuso Line:** Get off at Shin-Shibamata Station → Walk 12 minutes.

### 🚌 Bus Infrastructure
* **From JR Koiwa Station:** Board the Keisei Bus bound for Kanamachi → Travel approx. 15 minutes → Get off at "Shibamata Taishakuten" Bus Stop.
* **From JR Kanamachi Station:** Board the Keisei Bus bound for Koiwa → Travel approx. 5 minutes → Get off at "Shibamata Taishakuten" Bus Stop.

### 🚶 Walking Path from Shibamata Station
1. **Station Square Exit:** Exit the ticket gates into the main station square, which features permanent bronze statues of the cultural characters Tora-san and Sakura.
2. **Taishakuten Sando Path:** Move straight ahead with your back to the Tora-san statue along the commercial street lined with traditional food and souvenir shops.
3. **Main Gate Arrival:** The commercial path terminates directly at the wooden Nitenmon gate of the temple.

## 🍽️ Local Foods
![Main Approach to Temple](/images/prefectures/tokyo/shibamata_temple_approach.webp)

### 🍡 Traditional Comestibles along the Approach
The Sando approach features a dense cluster of long-established food and beverage operators. The regional food profile includes:
* **Kusa-dango:** Mugwort-infused rice dumplings, serving as the primary signature sweet of the local district.
* **River Fish Dishes:** Local specialty kitchens serving freshwater fish sourced or themed historically around the regional river networks.
* **Tempura:** Deep-fried seafood and vegetable dishes served by historic restaurants along the path.
* **Senbei:** Traditional baked rice crackers sold directly from storefronts, characterizing the historic atmosphere of the traditional-town (Shitamachi) commerce.

## 🏡 Landmarks & Garden Assets

### 🌲 Zuiryu no Matsu (Pine Tree)
![Zuiryu no Matsu Historic Pine Tree](/images/prefectures/tokyo/zuiryu_pine_tree.webp)
A large, historic pine tree located to the left of the main Taishakudo hall. Its central trunk grows vertically while its large branches extend north, south, and west, resembling a climbing dragon. According to temple records, the founder Nichiei Shonin chose this specific site in 1629 after finding this unique tree next to a natural freshwater spring.

### 🌿 Daikyoji Suikeien (Japanese Garden)
![Suikeien Traditional Landscape Garden](/images/prefectures/tokyo/daikyoji_suikeien.webp)
A 2,000-square-meter temple landscape garden located on the eastern side of the complex.
* **Design History:** First mapped around 1926, it was substantially redesigned by master landscape architect Nagai Rakuzan between the early Showa period and 1965.
* **Structural Layout:** A central pond occupies the northern half, flanked by an artificial hill (Tsukiyama) in the northwest and an island (Nakajima) in the northeast. Water flows down a two-tiered waterfall, moving through a curving stream to create a deep valley effect.
* **Optical Illusion Design:** The southern grass garden is elevated higher than the viewing hall (Daikyakuden). This optical design makes the grass appear closer and gives a sense of depth to the pond behind it.
* **Material Features:** Uses natural materials including Seto-Mikage stone, Kamo River red stone, and natural stone basins from the Katsura River. The "Futou-an" teahouse sits on the southeastern edge.

## ⚠️ Notes & Operational Rules

### 🚶 Sequential Worship Protocol
1. **Nitenmon Gate:** Enter through the main cedar gate built in 1896, which houses statues of the guardian deities Zochoten and Komokuten.
2. **Goshinshui Water:** Cleanse your hands at the natural spring water font located on the left side after passing through the gate.
3. **Taishakudo Hall:** Enter the interior hall where the central deity is housed inside a closed shrine box (Mizushi). Visitors seeking a temple seal (Goshuin) must request it after completing praying.
4. **Wood Carving Gallery:** Walk through the exhibition area showcasing complex wood carvings created by master woodworkers from the Meiji to Showa periods.
5. **Suikeien Garden:** Complete the route by walking through the covered wooden walkways around the landscape garden.
